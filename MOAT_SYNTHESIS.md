*Distilled from MARKET_ANALYSIS.md + MOAT_ANALYSIS.md, Aug 2026. Pricing/sizing details live there; this is the verdict layer.*

**Verdict:** PumpRun has **no moat today** — 8 demo customers, zero reviews, Django CRUD + NN routing replicable in weeks. Everything defensible is prospective. ## 2. PumpRun's Current Moat Map

**Defensible today (small but real):**
- **Town-by-town compliance content** — genuinely unclaimed. Dies in a weekend if written as marketing copy; survives only if maintained as a dataset (effective dates, change logs).
- **Statutory due-date spine** (14-day BOH filing clock) — a framing no generic FSM will copy for ~400 MA shops.
- **Flat $499 at 4+ trucks + rate-lock pitch** against Tank Track's weeks-old +20% hike — a position, not a moat, but evidenced and immediately usable.

**Thin — call these what they are:**
- **Code:** zero technical moat. Weeks to replicate.
- **Switching costs:** moderate at best — migrating a due-date ledger hurts, doesn't hurt *enough*. Until the ledger is the shop's audit trail and route-sale asset, churn stays a price conversation.
- **Brand/distribution:** 8 demo customers vs Tank Track's 300+ operators and a Pumper Nation seat. **This is the binding constraint, not features.**
- **Offline tap-complete:** real daily pain, real differentiator, replicable feature — a six-month head start, not a castle.

## 3. Three Moat-Building Moves (solo-executable)

### Move 1 — Data gravity: make the tank ledger the shop's balance sheet
Every tank keyed to parcel/address with pump-out history, gallons, drop tests, disposal manifests — plus a one-click **route-sale package** export. Route buy/sells are real events in this trade; being the system of record for diligence makes leaving expensive and staying profitable. Incumbents track *customer* recurrences; nobody treats tank history as an asset. Portability-on-your-terms also weaponizes anti-lock-in positioning against ServiceTitan's documented egress hostility.
**Profit impact:** churn is the only lever that matters. At 3%/mo churn, equilibrium ≈ 100 customers (~$534k/yr); at 2%/mo it's ≈ 150 (~$800k/yr). **One point of churn ≈ $265k/yr at scale**, and LTV rises from ~$14.9k to ~$22.5k per logo. Costs dev hours, not cash — highest ROI move available.

### Move 2 — Distribution: reference density in New England before national anything
Twelve named, callable customers with published outcomes outranks the entire vertical's third-party review base (~520 reviews combined; Tank Track and PumpDocket have zero). Execute via comparison-content SEO on exact buyer-research terms ("Tank Track vs," "ServiceCore alternative") — the review infrastructure is empty enough to own — and earn Pumper Nation presence through customer advocates. Never advertise there first.
**Profit impact:** cold-call CAC is ~$1,000–1,250 loaded founder time (~20 hrs/close); referral + comparison-content CAC drops toward the $100–150 cash floor. Halving founder-hours-per-close doubles monthly closes on identical hours — payback stays <1 month either way, but equilibrium scales with close rate. Founder hours are the binding constraint, so **distribution efficiency IS profit**.

### Move 3 — Compliance content as a maintained dataset, not copy
Per-jurisdiction filing rules with effective dates, change logs, deadline clocks, filing receipts, board-formatted PDFs. Scope discipline: **New England first (MA/NH/ME/CT/RI)**, not 50 states — PumpDocket's citation pages prove breadth-without-maintenance reads as marketing. Verified gap: Tank Track ships TX/OH only (publicly missed FL), PumpDocket disclaims compliance, ServiceCore has nothing. Perpetual boring maintenance is the moat — a $99 rival won't fund it for a 400-shop state.
**Profit impact:** this is the price-defense layer that converts "$499 vs $99" into "compliance insurance vs trip-ticket generator." It's why price never has to be the pitch, protects ~90% gross margin, and anchors the rate-lock offer. Each point of price retention across 50 customers ≈ $30k/yr.

## 4. Kill Criteria / Where NOT to Invest

**Pre-committed kill switches:**
- $299 drop-test converts **<10% of demos** → the 1–2-truck majority is unprofitable to serve; stay 3+ trucks only.
- Churn **>5%/mo after 6 months** → thesis dead regardless of pipeline; the market has priced compliance at $99.
- **MA centralizes e-filing** into a state portal → PDF wedge narrows fast; pivot trigger to multi-state town-level rules or wind down.

**Do-not-invest list:**
- **Network effects / marketplace / lead-gen** — Jobber refuses it for good reason; Tank Track owns the community channel; solo founders lose flywheels.
- **Payments/fintech attach** — municipal/net-terms billing produces no card spread; HCP's and Workiz's own economics prove it.
- **Feature work for 1–2-truck shops** before the $299 test proves they're servable.
- **Price competition vs Service Fusion** — their $208 flat undercuts you permanently; out-comply, never discount.
- **AI add-ons** (Workiz's game), **routing R&D beyond NN** (dispatch-dense optimization is ServiceCore/Titan turf), native-app rewrites, national SEO before New England density.
- **Fundraising** — category ceiling ~$27M ARR; this is a profit business, not a fund.

**Bottom line:** the moat is not code and not price. It's accumulated, *maintained* compliance truth plus a tank ledger shops won't abandon — acquired through references faster than Tank Track can react and before PumpDocket gets a review base. Every hour and dollar goes to those three moves; everything else is decoration.
